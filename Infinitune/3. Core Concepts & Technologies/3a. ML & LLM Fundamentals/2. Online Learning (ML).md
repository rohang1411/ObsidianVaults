

## 1. Simple Explanation (Analogy)

Online Learning is a method of training a model *incrementally* on a *stream* of data, rather than all at once.

**Analogy: Studying for an Exam**

* **Batch Learning (The Traditional Way):** You are given a 1000-page textbook at the start of the semester. You spend the last week of the semester (the "training phase") cramming—reading the entire book, making all your notes, and building a "model" of the content. You then take the final exam. You can't learn anything new *during* the semester. If a new discovery is made, you won't know it for the exam.

* **Online Learning (The InfiniTune Way):** You are given *one page* of the textbook every day. You read that one page, update your mental model, and then *throw the page away*. By the time the final exam comes, you have learned from all 1000 pages, but you did it incrementally.
    * **The Big Advantage:** If a new discovery is made (a new data point arrives), it's just "page 1001." You read it, update your knowledge, and are *immediately* ready for a pop quiz on it.

## 2. Why It Was Used in InfiniTune

**Online learning is the entire premise of InfiniTune.**

The project report identifies a key problem: traditional models "use batch data to train," but a "continuous stream of data wouldn't fit with batch training". This requires a switch to "alternative online learning methods".

InfiniTune is designed to be an *online learning framework*.
1.  The `Data Generator` creates the "one page per day" (the data stream).
2.  The `QLORA Trainer` is the "student" who reads that one page and updates their notes.
3.  This directly solves the "model staleness" problem by allowing the LLM to learn from new data *as it arrives*, rather than waiting for a massive "batch" retraining session.

## 3. Pros & Cons

| Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Adapts to New Data:** The model can adapt to evolving data in real-time. This is its single biggest advantage and the core of InfiniTune. | **Catastrophic Forgetting:** The model can "forget" old knowledge as it over-learns new patterns. (e.g., after reading 100 pages *only* about movies, it might get worse at history). |
| **Memory Efficient:** You don't need to store the entire massive dataset. You just need the *current* model and the *new* data point (or mini-batch). | **Less Stable:** Training can be "noisier" or less stable, as each update is based on a very small sample of data, not the whole dataset. |
| **Fast Updates:** The model is always up-to-date. InfiniTune achieved a ~60s update latency. | **Concept Drift:** Can be hard to manage if the *nature* of the data stream changes over time (e.g., switches from movie reviews to financial news). |
